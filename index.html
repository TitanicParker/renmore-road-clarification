Local residents should not be written out

Did the local paper serve the developer’s frame?

Residents objected to apartments beside their homes. Galway City Council agreed with them. Then the developer appealed. When the story reached the newspaper, the people and the neighbourhood were blurred into a softer “Lough Atalia area” story.

The story, from the start

A house became a planning fight

There was a house at 58 Renmore Road.

For years, it was not an asset, a site, or a development opportunity. It was someone’s home. An older woman lived there. She was a neighbour to the people around her. She passed away.

After her death, the house was inherited by her nephew, who lived in Dublin. He did not intend to live in it, develop it, or manage it himself. So the house was sold.

Then came the planning application.

In 2025, permission was sought for a two-storey building containing four apartments. This was not proposed in an empty field or a detached commercial zone. It was proposed at 58 Renmore Road, beside existing homes, close to local roads, and beside Dun na Mara.

The nearest neighbours objected. They objected because they were the people who would live with the consequences. They raised concerns about density, residential character, overlooking, local amenity, and the effect on the homes immediately around the site.

Galway City Council agreed. That is the important point. The objections were not brushed aside. They were sustained. The Council refused permission.

Then the developer appealed. For residents, things went quiet.

Last Friday, the Galway City Tribune reported that the apartments were “good to go on appeal”. But the article did not centre the neighbourhood that had objected. Instead of plainly saying this was 58 Renmore Road beside Dun na Mara, the article framed it as a development in the “Lough Atalia area”.

That is the issue: a local neighbourhood objection was abstracted into a softer development story.

Why this feels loaded

“Lough Atalia area” is broader, softer, and more anonymous. It can make the development sound like part of a general urban corridor where apartments are expected.

But this was not abstract. It was a house in a neighbourhood. It was beside people’s homes. It was objected to by the people living closest to it. And the Council agreed with those objections.

The concern is that the local newspaper carried a framing that serves the developer’s side of the story: make the site sound less residential, make the dispute sound more bureaucratic, and make the affected citizens less visible.

The frame changed from “residents objected and the Council agreed” to “apartments approved on appeal in the Lough Atalia area.” Those are not the same story.

1. The location was softened

“58 Renmore Road, beside Dun na Mara” became “the Lough Atalia area”. That changes the reader’s mental picture.

2. Citizens were pushed aside

The people living closest to the site become background detail, even though their objections were central to the original refusal.

3. The developer’s outcome was normalised

The successful appeal is made to look routine, while the neighbourhood objection that was upheld by the Council is diluted.

What we are asking for

We are not asking the newspaper to take our side. We are asking it to tell the story plainly.

Say where the site is. Say that it is 58 Renmore Road. Say that it is beside Dun na Mara. Say that residents objected. Say that Galway City Council refused permission. Say that the developer appealed and won.

Do not soften the location. Do not abstract away the affected people. Do not turn authentic neighbourhood objections into a detached bureaucratic story.
