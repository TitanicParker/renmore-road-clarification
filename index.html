58 Renmore Road: the story should not be abstracted away

This is the story from the beginning. A neighbour lived in a house. She passed away. The house was sold. A planning application followed. Local residents objected, and Galway City Council agreed with them. Then the developer appealed. When the appeal outcome reached the newspaper, the story was reframed as a “Lough Atalia area” development, rather than a Renmore Road development affecting the people who live beside it.

Three-minute read

What happened

There was a house at 58 Renmore Road.

For years, it was not an asset, a site, or a development opportunity. It was someone’s home. An older woman lived there. She was a neighbour to the people around her. She passed away.

After her death, the house was inherited by her nephew, who lived in Dublin. He did not intend to live in it, develop it, or manage it himself. So the house was sold.

Then came the planning application.

In 2025, permission was sought for a two-storey building containing four apartments. This was not proposed in an empty field or a detached commercial zone. It was proposed at 58 Renmore Road, beside existing homes, close to local roads, and beside Dun na Mara.

The nearest neighbours objected. They objected because they were the people who would live with the consequences. They raised concerns about density, residential character, overlooking, local amenity, and the effect on the homes immediately around the site.

Galway City Council agreed. That is the important point. The objections were not brushed aside. They were sustained. The Council refused permission.

Then the developer appealed. For residents, things went quiet.

Last Friday, the Galway City Tribune reported that the apartments were “good to go on appeal”. But the way the article described the matter changed the story. Instead of plainly saying this was 58 Renmore Road, beside Dun na Mara, where local residents had objected and had been upheld by the Council, the article framed it as a development in the “Lough Atalia area”.

That is the issue. The local reality was abstracted away.

Why that wording matters

“Lough Atalia area” is a broader, softer, more anonymous description. It can make the development sound like part of a general urban area where apartment development might seem ordinary or expected.

But this was not abstract. It was not merely a planning file. It was a house in a neighbourhood. It was a site beside people’s homes. It was objected to by the people living closest to it. And the Council agreed with those objections.

The concern is that the local newspaper, whether deliberately or not, helped move the story away from the people most affected.

It changed the frame from “Residents objected to a development beside their homes, and the Council agreed” to “A planning appeal succeeded for apartments in the Lough Atalia area.” Those are not the same story.

1. The location was softened

“58 Renmore Road, beside Dun na Mara” became “the Lough Atalia area”. That changes the reader’s mental picture.

2. The residents were displaced

The people living closest to the site become background detail, even though their objections were central to the original refusal.

3. The story became bureaucratic

The dispute is made to sound like a jostle between institutions, rather than a development beside homes after local objections were sustained.

What we are asking for

We are not asking the newspaper to take our side. We are asking it to tell the story plainly.

Say where the site is. Say that it is 58 Renmore Road. Say that it is beside Dun na Mara. Say that residents objected. Say that Galway City Council refused permission. Say that the developer appealed and won.

Do not soften the location. Do not abstract away the affected people. Do not turn a local residential dispute into a detached bureaucratic story.
